# Finding research publications

Use several sources, as no single index provides complete coverage. Suggested search terms include:

- `mutation testing`
- `mutation analysis`
- `mutant selection`
- `equivalent mutants`
- `higher order mutation`
- `mutation-based test generation`
- `mutation testing tools`

## Discovery indexes

- [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) — broad discovery, citation chaining, and versions hosted by authors.
- [DBLP](https://dblp.org/) — reliable computer-science publication and author metadata, especially for conference papers.
- [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/) — topic discovery, related-paper recommendations, and citation graphs.
- [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/) — an open scholarly catalogue with filters and an API for repeatable searches.
- [Crossref](https://search.crossref.org/) — DOI discovery and verification of publication metadata.
- [The Lens](https://www.lens.org/) — scholarly search and citation exploration.
- [Dimensions](https://www.dimensions.ai/) — publications, citations, grants, datasets, and patents.
- [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/) — open-access papers aggregated from institutional repositories and journals.
- [BASE](https://www.base-search.net/) — an academic repository search service with strong open-access coverage.
- [OpenAIRE Explore](https://explore.openaire.eu/) — European research outputs, datasets, software, and projects.
- [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/) — literature and preprints relevant when mutation testing intersects with bioinformatics or scientific software.
- [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/) — publications from vetted open-access journals.
- [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/) — journals, books, and older scholarly material; software-engineering coverage is more limited.
- [ProQuest](https://www.proquest.com/) — dissertations and theses; access may require an institutional subscription.
- [WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/) — books, proceedings, theses, and library holdings.
- [Google Dataset Search](https://datasetsearch.research.google.com/) — mutation-testing datasets and reproduction artefacts rather than papers.
- [Scopus](https://www.scopus.com/) and [Web of Science](https://www.webofscience.com/) — curated citation indexes; access may require an institutional subscription.

## Citation and related-paper discovery

- [ResearchRabbit](https://www.researchrabbit.ai/) — explores related authors, papers, and citation networks.
- [Connected Papers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/) — generates a similarity graph from a seed paper.
- [Litmaps](https://www.litmaps.com/) — citation mapping and alerts for newly published work.
- [Inciteful](https://inciteful.xyz/) — open citation-network exploration.
- [Elicit](https://elicit.com/) — AI-assisted literature discovery and structured review.

Use these services to find candidates. Before adding an entry, confirm its title, authors, year, and identifiers against a DOI record, publisher page, or another authoritative source.

## Open repositories

- [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/) — preprints, including work not yet indexed by publishers.
- [HAL](https://hal.science/) — open manuscripts and research outputs.
- [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/) — papers, datasets, and reproduction packages.
- [Open Science Framework](https://osf.io/) — preprints, project materials, datasets, and reproducibility packages.
- [Figshare](https://figshare.com/) — papers, datasets, figures, and supplementary research materials.
- [Dataverse](https://dataverse.org/) — research datasets and replication packages hosted by participating institutions.
- [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/) — curated research data linked to scholarly publications.
- [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/) — working papers and preprints, including computer-science research.
- [TechRxiv](https://www.techrxiv.org/) — engineering and computer-science preprints.
- [engrXiv](https://engrxiv.org/) — engineering preprints hosted by the Open Engineering community.
- [SciELO](https://scielo.org/) — open-access journals and research from multiple countries and disciplines.
- [Software Heritage](https://www.softwareheritage.org/) — archived source code for tools and reproducibility artefacts.
- [GitHub](https://github.com/) and [GitLab](https://gitlab.com/) — source code, datasets, and reproduction packages supplied by authors.
- Institutional and author repositories — legally available manuscripts when access to the publisher's copy is restricted.

Prefer the DOI or publisher page for catalogue entries. Add a repository copy only when its redistribution or hosting is authorised.

### Surveys and systematic reviews

- [An Analysis and Survey of the Development of Mutation Testing](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2010.62)
- [Mutation Testing Advances: An Analysis and Survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.adcom.2018.03.015)
- [A Systematic Literature Review of Techniques and Metrics to Reduce the Cost of Mutation Testing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2019.07.100)
- [A Systematic Literature Review of How Mutation Testing Supports Quality Assurance Processes](https://doi.org/10.1002/stvr.1675)
- Reference lists from other surveys and systematic reviews in the catalogue

Use seed collections for backward citation searches, then use Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, or another citation index for forward citation searches.

## Suggested process

1. Search DBLP and at least one broad discovery index.
2. Follow citations in relevant surveys and foundational papers.
3. Confirm metadata on the DOI record or publisher page.
4. Search the repository by title and DOI to avoid duplicates.
5. Add directly relevant papers to `README.md` in reverse chronological order.
